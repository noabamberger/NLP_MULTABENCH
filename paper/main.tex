\begin{abstract}
We curate and evaluate two new text--tabular datasets against the MulTaBench curation
criterion, using the official MulTaBench implementation of five tabular learners (TabM,
CatBoost, LightGBM, TabPFN v2, TabPFN 2.5) under four conditions. Both pass.
\emph{Vietnam housing} passes on \textbf{5 of 5} learners with a joint-signal gain of
$+0.288 \pm 0.024$ $R^2$ pooled over 25 (learner, fold) cells, positive in every cell
($t = 59.9$); \emph{Udemy courses} passes on \textbf{3 of 5} with $+0.175 \pm 0.046$, also
positive in every cell ($t = 19.1$) --- two orders of magnitude above the acceptance margin
$\delta = 0.001$. We report the target-awareness term with equal candour: it is the binding
constraint, clearly significant on Vietnam housing ($t = 4.7$) and \emph{not} significant when
pooled on Udemy ($t = 0.9$) despite meeting the per-learner criterion. We also report failed
candidates, because two failed in ways a naive pipeline would score as successes: deleting
structured columns by name \emph{manufactures} a joint gain that vanishes when they are
restored ($+0.0388 \rightarrow -0.0005$), and cheap screens of the target-awareness term are
invalid along two independent axes. All measurements were produced on Kaggle T4 GPUs, with all
four conditions for a learner measured inside a single session.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Text--tabular learning concerns tables in which some columns hold numerical or categorical
features and others hold free text. MulTaBench~\cite{multabench} formalises when such a
dataset is worth benchmarking: the text and the structured block must each contribute
predictive signal the other lacks, and a text encoder adapted to the prediction target must
improve on a frozen one. A dataset satisfying both conditions exercises the capability that
text--tabular models are built for; a dataset failing either collapses into a pure tabular
task or a pure NLP task.

This paper takes the benchmark track: we identify, preprocess and evaluate new datasets
against that criterion rather than proposing a new algorithm. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Two new passing datasets} from domains absent from MulTaBench --- Vietnamese
residential real-estate listings (5 of 5 learners) and online-course listings (3 of 5) ---
with joint gains of $+0.288$ and $+0.175$ $R^2$, positive in all 25 (learner, fold) cells for
both.
\item \textbf{An automated mining pipeline} and its measured yield: 232 candidates discovered,
2 surviving scrutiny, with the survival rate at every stage reported so the funnel can be
sized by others.
\item \textbf{Two negative methodological results} that invalidate the obvious cheap
pipeline. Name-based deletion of structured columns manufactures joint signal; and the
target-awareness term cannot be screened cheaply along either the epoch axis or the fold
axis, because cheapening it collapses the quantity being measured.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Curation Criterion}

Following MulTaBench, each dataset is evaluated with five learners --- TabM~\cite{tabm},
CatBoost~\cite{catboost}, LightGBM~\cite{lightgbm}, TabPFN v2~\cite{tabpfnv2} and TabPFN
2.5~\cite{tabpfn25} --- under four conditions: \textbf{Unimodal Structured} (tabular columns
only), \textbf{Unimodal Unstructured} (text only), \textbf{Joint Frozen} (all features, with
frozen E5-small-v2~\cite{e5} embeddings), and \textbf{Joint Target-Aware} (TAR; all features,
with E5 LoRA-tuned~\cite{lora} on the target, as in TabSTAR~\cite{tabstar}). Text columns are
embedded per column and compressed to 30 principal components. Scores are $R^2$ for
regression; both our datasets are regression tasks.

Writing $\mu_c$ for the mean score over folds $0$--$4$ in condition $c$, rounded to three
decimals before differencing, a learner passes when
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\text{Joint}} &= \mu_{\text{frozen}} - \max(\mu_{\text{struct}}, \mu_{\text{text}}) > \delta,\\
\Delta_{\text{Aware}} &= \mu_{\text{TAR}} - \mu_{\text{frozen}} > \delta,
\end{align}
with $\delta = 0.001$. A dataset is accepted when at least 3 of the 5 learners pass
\emph{both} inequalities. A learner clearing one inequality but not the other contributes
nothing. Every verdict in this paper is computed by the MulTaBench reference implementation of
this rule, which asserts grid completeness (5 folds $\times$ 4 conditions per learner) rather
than averaging over gaps; we did not reimplement the arithmetic.

\paragraph{Experimental setup.} All runs were executed on Kaggle T4 GPUs. Because each
$\Delta$ is a difference of two means, the four conditions for a given learner are always
measured inside a single GPU session, so that no environment difference can enter a delta.
Each dataset's grid is $5 \times 4 \times 5 = 100$ cells, costing $0.65$ GPU-hours for Udemy and
$1.87$ for the ten-times-larger Vietnam housing: cost scales with training-set size, not with
raw row count, since evaluation subsamples to at most 10k training rows. TAR fine-tuning is
shared across learners within a fold --- the tuned encoder is a function of the training texts
and labels, not of the downstream learner --- which reduces a 25-cell TAR sweep to 10
fine-tunings.

\section{Method: Mining and Validating Candidates}

\paragraph{Funnel and yield.} Candidates were discovered by programmatic search over public
dataset repositories, then profiled, screened and gridded. Table~\ref{tab:funnel} gives the
measured survival at each stage. The end-to-end yield is under $1\%$, which is the single most
useful number for anyone sizing such an effort: reaching five accepted datasets implies
screening on the order of 500 candidates.

\paragraph{Automated specification.} For each candidate the pipeline selects a target and
partitions columns without human annotation. Targets are rejected when they are identifiers or
serial numbers, when one value holds more than $20\%$ of the mass (a missing-value sentinel
masquerading as a number), when $|z|_{\max} > 5$, or when the target is derived from the text
it would be predicted from. Columns correlating with the target at Spearman $\rho \ge 0.95$ are
dropped as leakage; Spearman rather than Pearson, so that monotone-but-nonlinear leaks such as
a rank column are caught.

\paragraph{Validity rule 1: never delete structured columns by name.} A name-based ``junk''
filter that removes columns matching patterns like \texttt{year} or \texttt{time} is an obvious
convenience and it is actively harmful. On a board-games candidate, \texttt{Year Published} and
\texttt{Play Time} are not identifiers but the two strongest structured predictors. Deleting
them cripples the structured baseline and lets the text act as a \emph{proxy} for the deleted
feature, inventing a joint gain that disappears when they return:
\[
\Delta_{\text{Joint}} = +0.0388 \;\longrightarrow\; +0.0229 \;\longrightarrow\; -0.0005
\]
as the columns are restored one at a time. The unstructured baseline is unchanged at $0.502$
throughout, so this is entirely an effect on the structured baseline: a controlled ablation,
not a correlation. Consequently every positive $\Delta_{\text{Joint}}$ we report was re-measured
with the maximal structured block --- nothing dropped except proven leakage --- and a candidate
whose gain collapses under that test is discarded. Name patterns are used only to reject
\emph{text} candidates, never to remove structured columns.

\paragraph{Validity rule 2: $\Delta_{\text{Aware}}$ cannot be screened cheaply.} A cheap screen
is valid only where cheapness does not change the quantity being screened. This holds for
$\Delta_{\text{Joint}}$, which uses frozen encoders, so a subset of folds is unbiased. It fails
for $\Delta_{\text{Aware}}$ along two independent axes, and we measured both.
\emph{Epochs}: an under-trained LoRA adapter barely moves the encoder, so TAR $\approx$ frozen
and the delta collapses toward zero by construction. At 2 epochs one of our accepted datasets
scored $+0.0099$ (LightGBM, fold 0); the identical cell at 10 epochs scored $+0.0322$. A failure
at a low epoch budget therefore proves nothing; only a pass is informative. \emph{Folds}: a
board-games fold-0 screen of $+0.0074$ to $+0.0163$ reversed to $-0.0003$ to $+0.0021$ on the
full five-fold grid, two learners changing sign; an anime fold-0 screen of $+0.002$/$+0.003$
became 0 of 5. The per-(learner, fold) spread is $\sigma \approx 0.006$, roughly six times
$\delta$, so one fold cannot resolve a criterion whose threshold sits inside its noise band. We
therefore evaluate $\Delta_{\text{Aware}}$ only at $\ge 10$ epochs over all five folds.

\paragraph{Vietnam housing (\textsc{reg\_text\_houses\_vietnam\_2024}).} Residential
for-sale listings; 30{,}229 rows. The target is \texttt{Price} (687 distinct values,
$|z|_{\max} = 2.54$, no outlier clipping required); the single text column is the free-form
\texttt{Address}; the structured block is physical attributes (area, frontage, floors,
bedrooms, bathrooms, orientation, legal status, furnishing). \textbf{Accepted, 5 of 5.} Pooled over the 25 (learner,
fold) cells, $\Delta_{\text{Joint}} = 0.288 \pm 0.024$, positive in \textbf{25 of 25} cells,
$t = 59.9$; $\Delta_{\text{Aware}} = 0.0065 \pm 0.0068$, positive in 22 of 25, $t = 4.7$. The
joint gain nearly doubles the better unimodal baseline ($0.35 \rightarrow 0.65$).

The mechanism is interpretable and is, we believe, the property worth searching for directly.
The address encodes \emph{location premium} --- street, ward, district, city --- while the
structured block encodes \emph{physical size and condition}. These are orthogonal drivers of
price and neither is derivable from the other, which is precisely why the joint condition beats
both unimodal baselines by so much. The two baselines are also nearly balanced ($0.34$ vs
$0.33$ on average), the fingerprint of two independently informative channels rather than one
dominant modality with a decorative second.

\paragraph{Udemy courses (\textsc{reg\_text\_edu\_udemy\_academy}).} Online-course listings;
2{,}999 rows. The target is course price (28 distinct values, $|z|_{\max} = 3.00$, no extreme
outliers); the text columns are \texttt{course\_name} and \texttt{course\_instr}; the
structured block is six numeric course statistics. No course or MOOC dataset exists in
MulTaBench, so this is a genuinely new domain. \textbf{Accepted, 3 of 5} (CatBoost, TabPFN v2,
TabPFN 2.5). $\Delta_{\text{Joint}} = 0.175 \pm 0.046$ pooled, positive in \textbf{25 of 25}
cells, $t = 19.1$; both unimodal baselines are non-degenerate ($\approx 0.30$ structured,
$\approx 0.21$ text), so the joint gain is genuine complementarity rather than one modality
collapsing.

We state the weakness plainly. Udemy's $\Delta_{\text{Aware}}$ meets the per-learner criterion
on exactly the quorum of three, but pooled over all 25 cells it is $0.0036 \pm 0.0202$ with
$t = 0.9$ --- \emph{not} significant, and negative on two learners. The dataset's joint signal
is beyond dispute; its target-awareness signal is marginal and rests on CatBoost ($+0.021$) and
TabPFN 2.5 ($+0.012$), the only two learners clearly above the noise. Vietnam housing is the
stronger of our two contributions on this axis, and is the one we would nominate for inclusion
in a combined benchmark.

\paragraph{Negative results.} Of four candidates gridded, two failed, and both are informative.
\emph{Board games} showed a healthy $\Delta_{\text{Joint}}$ ($+0.047$ to $+0.059$ once the
structured block was restored) and then died on target-awareness at 1 of 5, counting its
knife-edge cell honestly. \emph{Anime popularity} likewise cleared the joint condition
($+0.031$ to $+0.037$) and failed target-awareness at 0 of 5, a LoRA-tuned encoder scoring
\emph{worse} than a frozen one on three of four learners. A Metacritic-derived table was
rejected at the target: $82\%$ of its values were a single sentinel zero, so the apparent
regression task was really a has-a-score indicator. The pattern is consistent and is itself a
finding: the joint condition is comparatively easy --- 16 of 34 screened candidates cleared it
--- while target-awareness eliminates most of them.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Epoch budget.} TAR fine-tuning ran 10 epochs rather than the reference default of 50
with patience 3. This is conservative in the direction that matters: $\Delta_{\text{Aware}}$
grew monotonically with the epoch budget in every measurement we made (the $+0.0099 \rightarrow
+0.0322$ comparison above), so the full budget would be expected to widen our accepted margins.
It cannot manufacture a pass, only suppress one.

\textbf{Deltas are not comparable across datasets.} $R^2$ deltas depend on intrinsic task
difficulty; the scale-free ratio of joint to best-unimodal performance ($1.85\times$ for Vietnam
housing, $1.61\times$ for Udemy, averaged over learners) is the more transferable statistic.

\textbf{Two datasets, not five.} We meet the standard scope twice over but not the outstanding
scope; the measured $<1\%$ yield in Table~\ref{tab:funnel} quantifies what reaching five costs.

\section{Conclusion}

We contribute two new text--tabular datasets that pass the MulTaBench curation criterion --- one
at 5 of 5 learners with a joint gain of $+0.288$ $R^2$ positive in every measured cell, the
other at 3 of 5 with $+0.175$ --- both two orders of magnitude above the acceptance margin.
Equally, we report that target-awareness rather than joint signal is what eliminates candidates,
that it cannot be screened cheaply along either the epoch or the fold axis, and that a
name-based structured-column filter manufactures joint signal that does not exist. The most
productive question when assessing a candidate is not whether it contains text, but whether the
text describes a \emph{different aspect of the entity} than the structured columns do: both of
our passing datasets have that property, and restatement is precisely where the joint gain
vanishes.
