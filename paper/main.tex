{\small
We curate and evaluate two new text--tabular datasets against the MulTaBench curation
criterion, using the official implementation of five tabular learners (TabM, CatBoost,
LightGBM, TabPFN v2, TabPFN-2.5) under four conditions. Both are accepted. \emph{Vietnam
housing} (30k residential listings, address text) passes on \textbf{5 of 5} learners with a
joint-signal gain of $+0.288 \pm 0.024$ $R^2$ pooled over 25 (learner, fold) cells, positive in
every cell ($t = 59.9$); \emph{Udemy courses} (3k course listings) passes on \textbf{3 of 5}
with $+0.175 \pm 0.046$, also positive in every cell ($t = 19.1$) --- both two orders of
magnitude above the acceptance margin $\delta = 0.001$. We report the target-awareness term
with equal candour: it is the binding constraint, significant on Vietnam housing ($t = 4.7$)
but not when pooled on Udemy ($t = 0.9$). We describe the mining pipeline that produced them
(232 candidates screened, 2 accepted, 380 measured cells over 8.1 GPU-hours) and two screening
shortcuts we measured to be invalid.
}

\section{Introduction}

Real tables are rarely purely numerical: a property listing carries an address, a course
listing a title and an instructor name --- free text beside numeric and categorical columns.
\emph{Text--tabular learning} asks how to use both channels at once. The question is not
rhetorical: gradient-boosted trees remain very strong on structured data
\cite{grinsztajn,shwartzziv}, so a multimodal method only earns its complexity on tables where
the text carries signal the structured block lacks, and vice versa.

That makes \emph{dataset curation} a research problem rather than bookkeeping. A benchmark
built from tables whose text merely restates their columns will report that multimodal methods
do not help --- a fact about the benchmark, not about the methods. MulTaBench \cite{multabench}
makes the requirement measurable: a table is worth benchmarking only if (i) the joint model
beats \emph{both} unimodal baselines, and (ii) a text encoder adapted to the prediction target
beats a frozen one. Failing (i) collapses the task into pure tabular or pure NLP; failing (ii)
exercises text encoding but not the target-aware representation learning that models such as
TabSTAR \cite{tabstar} are built around.

We take the benchmark track: we identify, preprocess and evaluate new datasets against that
criterion rather than proposing an algorithm. We contribute (1) \textbf{two new accepted
datasets} --- Vietnamese residential real-estate listings (5 of 5 learners) and online-course
listings (3 of 5), with joint gains of $+0.288$ and $+0.175$ $R^2$ positive in all 25 (learner,
fold) cells for both; (2) \textbf{the mining pipeline that found them} and its measured yield,
232 candidates to 2, reported stage by stage so the funnel can be sized by others; and (3)
\textbf{two negative methodological results} that invalidate the obvious cheap pipeline.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Tabular learning.} Gradient-boosted decision trees --- XGBoost \cite{xgboost},
LightGBM \cite{lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{catboost} --- are the default for tabular
prediction and remain competitive against deep architectures \cite{grinsztajn,shwartzziv}. Deep
tabular models have closed much of the gap: FT-Transformer \cite{fttransformer} adapted
attention to heterogeneous columns, and TabM \cite{tabm} reaches GBDT-level accuracy through
parameter-efficient ensembling of an MLP backbone. We use LightGBM, CatBoost and TabM.

\paragraph{Tabular foundation models.} TabPFN v2 \cite{tabpfnv2} performs in-context learning
over a synthetic prior, strong on small tables without per-dataset gradient training;
TabPFN-2.5 \cite{tabpfn25} extends it to larger inputs. Being pretrained rather than fitted,
they respond differently to a change of input representation than trees do --- which is why a
five-learner committee is the right instrument for a curation decision.

\paragraph{Text and tables together.} One line of work serialises rows into sentences for a
language model (TabLLM \cite{tabllm}); another embeds string entries as entities to transfer
across tables (CARTE \cite{carte}). The line we follow keeps the tabular learner and encodes
text columns into features with a contrastive sentence encoder, E5 \cite{e5}, optionally
adapted to the target. TabSTAR \cite{tabstar} makes that adaptation central ---
\emph{semantically target-aware} representations --- and MulTaBench operationalises it as the
\texttt{ft} condition: an E5 encoder LoRA-tuned \cite{lora} on the target.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and curation.} MulTaBench \cite{multabench} is, to our knowledge, the
first multimodal-tabular benchmark to publish an executable acceptance rule for its own
datasets rather than a curated list alone. We reuse its reference implementation of that rule
verbatim (Section~\ref{sec:method}), so our verdicts and the benchmark's are one computation.

\section{Method: the criterion, the pipeline, and the datasets}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{The criterion.} Each dataset is evaluated with five learners --- TabM, CatBoost,
LightGBM, TabPFN v2 and TabPFN-2.5 --- under four conditions: \textbf{Unimodal Structured}
(tabular columns only), \textbf{Unimodal Unstructured} (text only), \textbf{Joint Frozen} (all
features, frozen E5-small-v2 embeddings) and \textbf{Joint Target-Aware} (TAR; all features,
E5 LoRA-tuned on the target). Text columns are embedded per column and compressed to 30
principal components; scores are $R^2$, as both our datasets are regression tasks. Writing
$\mu_c$ for the mean score over folds $0$--$4$ in condition $c$, rounded to three decimals
before differencing, a learner passes when
\begin{align}
\dj &= \mu_{\text{frozen}} - \max(\mu_{\text{struct}}, \mu_{\text{text}}) > \delta,
&\da &= \mu_{\text{TAR}} - \mu_{\text{frozen}} > \delta,
\end{align}
with $\delta = 0.001$; a dataset is accepted when at least 3 of the 5 learners pass
\emph{both}, a learner clearing only one contributing nothing. Every verdict here is computed
by the MulTaBench reference implementation of this rule, which asserts grid completeness
(5 folds $\times$ 4 conditions per learner) rather than averaging over gaps; we did not
reimplement the arithmetic.

\paragraph{Setup and fidelity.} All grids ran on Kaggle T4 GPUs, the four conditions for a
learner always measured in one session so that no environment difference can enter a delta;
the four grids total 380 cells and 8.1 GPU-hours (Appendix~\ref{app:cost}). We first
reproduced the benchmark's own artefacts: its published $56 \times 10$ pass matrix re-derives
with \textbf{0 mismatched cells}, and on the paper's anchor dataset the encoder-free condition
reproduces published scores exactly while $\dj$ agrees within $0.012$, every sign matching
(Appendix~\ref{app:anchor}).

\paragraph{Mining pipeline.} Candidates were discovered by programmatic search over public
dataset repositories, then profiled, screened and gridded (Table~\ref{tab:funnel}); end-to-end
yield is under $1\%$, so reaching five accepted datasets implies screening on the order of 500
candidates. For each candidate the pipeline selects a target and partitions columns without
human annotation. Targets are rejected when they are identifiers or serial numbers, when one
value holds more than $20\%$ of the mass (a sentinel masquerading as a number), when
$|z|_{\max} > 5$, or when derived from the text they would be predicted from; columns
correlating with the target at Spearman $\rho \ge 0.95$ are dropped as leakage (Spearman, so
monotone-but-nonlinear leaks such as a rank column are caught). Column typing is inferred, not
declared --- a non-numeric column counts as text at $\ge 100$ distinct values or $\ge 80\%$
uniqueness --- and it fails mainly by promoting dates and identifiers into the text block,
which is what reduces 120 nominally viable candidates to 58. Two cost-saving shortcuts that
look obvious are measurably invalid (Appendix~\ref{app:validity}), so every positive $\dj$
below was re-measured with the maximal structured block and every $\da$ at $\ge 10$ epochs
over all five folds.

\paragraph{The two datasets.} \textsc{reg\_text\_houses\_vietnam\_2024}
(\texttt{nguyentiennhan/vietnam-housing-dataset-2024}): 30{,}229 residential for-sale listings;
target \texttt{Price} (687 distinct, $|z|_{\max} = 2.54$); one text column, the free-form
\texttt{Address}; structured block six numeric and four categorical physical attributes (area,
frontage, floors, bedrooms, bathrooms, orientation, legal status, furnishing), none of which
duplicates the address. \textsc{reg\_text\_edu\_udemy\_academy}
(\texttt{mariahalshiekh/udemy-course-academy-teaching}): 2{,}999 online-course listings; target
course price (28 distinct, $|z|_{\max} = 3.00$); text columns \texttt{course\_name} and
\texttt{course\_instr}; structured block six numeric course statistics. Both specifications
were derived by the automated pipeline above --- neither needed a hand-written annotation
module, and neither had a column dropped for leakage.

\paragraph{Vietnam housing --- accepted, 5 of 5.} Pooled over the 25 (learner, fold) cells
(Appendix~\ref{app:vietnam}), $\dj = 0.288 \pm 0.024$, positive in \textbf{25 of 25},
$t = 59.9$; $\da = 0.0065 \pm 0.0068$, positive in 22 of 25, $t = 4.7$. The joint condition
nearly doubles the better unimodal baseline ($0.35 \rightarrow 0.65$). The mechanism is
interpretable, and is the property we think worth searching for directly: the address encodes
\emph{location premium} while the structured block encodes \emph{physical size and condition}
--- orthogonal drivers of price, neither derivable from the other. The baselines are also
nearly balanced ($0.34$ vs $0.33$), the fingerprint of two independently informative channels.
\textbf{One caveat we state rather than bury}: MulTaBench already contains four housing
datasets. This is a different market and task, but the domain overlaps, and domain novelty is a
judgement separate from whether the criterion is met.

\paragraph{Udemy courses --- accepted, 3 of 5.} $\dj = 0.175 \pm 0.046$ pooled
(Appendix~\ref{app:udemy}), positive in \textbf{25 of 25}, $t = 19.1$; both unimodal baselines
are non-degenerate ($\approx 0.30$ structured, $\approx 0.21$ text), so the gain is genuine
complementarity rather than one modality collapsing. No course or MOOC dataset exists in
MulTaBench, so the domain is genuinely new. Two weaknesses, stated plainly. Pooled over all 25
cells $\da$ is $0.0036 \pm 0.0202$, $t = 0.9$ --- \emph{not} significant, and negative on two
learners --- though it meets the per-learner criterion on exactly the quorum of three. And we
measured the dataset twice on independent machines: both lanes accept at 3 of 5 and $\dj$
reproduces within $0.011$, but \emph{not by the same three learners}, LightGBM and TabPFN v2
flipping in opposite directions and cancelling (Appendix~\ref{app:lanes}). \textbf{The
dataset-level verdict is reproducible; a per-learner $\da$ on this dataset is not.}

\paragraph{Negative results.} Of four candidates gridded, two failed
(Figure~\ref{fig:scatter}, Appendix~\ref{app:rejected}). \emph{Board games} cleared the joint
condition ($+0.047$ to $+0.059$ with the structured block restored) and died on
target-awareness at 1 of 5. \emph{Anime popularity} likewise cleared it ($+0.031$ to $+0.037$)
and failed at 0 of 5; pooled, its $\da$ is $-0.0012$, $t = -2.15$ --- the tuned encoder is
\emph{significantly worse} than the frozen one. A Metacritic table was rejected at the target:
$82\%$ of rows carried a sentinel zero, so the regression was really a has-a-score indicator. A
fifth candidate, MTG card prices, has a complete frozen grid ($\dj = +0.050$ to $+0.075$) but no
measured $\da$ and is reported as unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:mtg}); screen-time rejections
are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:screen}. The pattern is itself a finding: joint signal is
comparatively easy --- 16 of 34 screened candidates cleared it --- while target-awareness
eliminates most.

\section{Conclusion and Summary}

We contribute two new text--tabular datasets that pass the MulTaBench curation criterion --- one
at 5 of 5 learners with a joint gain of $+0.288$ $R^2$ positive in every measured cell, the
other at 3 of 5 with $+0.175$ --- both two orders of magnitude above the acceptance margin and
both measured on complete 100-cell grids with no absent cells. Equally, we report that
target-awareness rather than joint signal is what eliminates candidates, that it cannot be
screened cheaply along either the epoch or the fold axis, and that a name-based
structured-column filter manufactures joint signal that does not exist. The most productive
question when assessing a candidate is not whether it contains text, but whether the text
describes a \emph{different aspect of the entity} than the structured columns do: both accepted
datasets have that property, and restatement is where the joint gain vanishes.

\paragraph{Limitations.} \emph{Epoch budget}: TAR fine-tuning ran 10 epochs rather than the
reference default of 50 with patience 3. This is conservative --- $\da$ grew monotonically with
the budget in every measurement we made --- so the full budget would widen our margins; it can
suppress a pass, not manufacture one. \emph{Comparability}: $R^2$ deltas depend on task
difficulty, so the scale-free ratio of joint to best-unimodal performance ($1.85\times$ Vietnam
housing, $1.61\times$ Udemy) transfers better across datasets. \emph{Reproducibility}: a
per-learner $\da$ near the threshold need not survive a change of machine, so only
dataset-level verdicts should be cited. \emph{Scope}: two datasets, not five --- the standard
scope twice over but not the outstanding scope, and the measured ${<}1\%$ yield in
Table~\ref{tab:funnel} quantifies what reaching five would cost.

\section{Validity evidence: the two invalid shortcuts}
\label{app:validity}

\noindent Two obvious ways to cut curation cost are measurably invalid, each because it
collapses the quantity being measured. \textbf{(1) Never delete structured columns by name.} A
junk filter dropping columns that match \texttt{year} or \texttt{time} removes \texttt{Year
Published} and \texttt{Play Time} from a board-games candidate --- not identifiers but its two
strongest structured predictors --- letting the text proxy for them and inventing a joint gain
that vanishes on restoration. The unstructured baseline never moves, so this is a controlled
ablation rather than a correlation; name patterns are therefore used only to reject \emph{text}
candidates. \textbf{(2) $\da$ cannot be screened cheaply.} A cheap screen is valid only where
cheapness does not change the quantity screened. That holds for $\dj$ (frozen encoders, so a
fold subset is unbiased) and fails for $\da$ on two independent axes: an under-trained adapter
drives TAR toward frozen by construction, and a single fold cannot resolve a threshold sitting
deep inside the per-(learner, fold) noise band. Screening on one fold cost two full grids.

\texttt{popular-video-games-1980-2023} & 1{,}512 rows; no numeric column qualified as a target \\
\texttt{lego-sets} & 12{,}261 rows are 744 products replicated across 21 countries --- near-duplicates straddle train and test \\
\texttt{top-women-chess-players} & $\dj = +0.024$ inside the $\pm 0.015$ band, text $R^2 = 0.074$ (text nearly inert) \\

\texttt{daraz-perfumes} & every state negative ($-0.130$ / $-0.009$ / $-0.045$): a delta between them is meaningless \\

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Two classes recur. The first is a junk target chosen automatically: \texttt{appid},
\texttt{Sl No} and \texttt{serial\_no} are primary keys or serial numbers, and one candidate
had a release date selected as its target. The second is a saturated or degenerate baseline
against which no text signal can be demonstrated, with structured $R^2$ reaching $0.969$,
$0.983$, $0.997$ and $1.000$ on four separate candidates. Both classes are cheap to detect from
a profile of the target and the structured block, and neither needs a grid to rule out --- which
is what makes the profiling stage of the funnel worth its cost.

\noindent All four conditions for a given learner share one session, so no environment difference can
enter a delta. The four gridded candidates cost 8.07 GPU-hours over 380 cells in total. Cost is
not proportional to row count: evaluation subsamples to at most 10k training rows, so the
remaining variation is driven by text length and by the learner mix rather than by dataset
size. Vietnam housing has $1.5\times$ the rows of board games and costs half as much. The
practical consequence for anyone sizing this work is that the grid is the cheap part --- under
two GPU-hours for an accepted dataset --- and the screening that precedes it is where the
effort goes.
